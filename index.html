About

I trained as a telecommunications engineer and spent three years advising clients on Cisco infrastructure before moving into teaching. That mix of industry practice and classroom work shapes how I design labs: every exercise begins with a problem engineers actually face, such as a misconfigured router, a weak firewall rule or a program that fails on an edge case.

I now lead practical sessions at university level while completing my Master's degree. My current interest is the security of machine learning systems, and how to prepare students to work on it.

Research interests

  • AI security. How machine learning systems fail, and practical ways to harden them.
  • Network security. VLAN security, intrusion detection, firewalls and evasion techniques.
  • Malware analysis and cryptography. Understanding threats in order to teach defence.
  • Routing and network design. RIP, EIGRP, OSPF and BGP, with IPv4 and IPv6.
  • Computing education. Laboratory design, practical assessment and feedback.

Teaching approach

Learn by building

Students configure, program and test real systems in the lab. Theory arrives through the problem, not before it.

Assess by doing

Progress is measured through lab assignments, projects and practical exams, with individual feedback at each stage.

Keep materials current

I co-design exercises with faculty and update material as curricula and industry standards change, as I did when revising CCNA content after Cisco's certification updates.

Support each student

I mentor students individually toward their coursework and certification goals.

Hash generator

    

    
The same input always gives the same hash, and changing one character changes the whole result. SHA-1 is included for teaching only; it is no longer collision-resistant.

  
Password entropy estimator

    

    
Entropy = length × log₂(size of character pool). This assumes random characters, so passwords built from common words or patterns are weaker than shown. Do not type a real password.

  
CPU scheduling simulator

    

    
Enter burst times for P1, P2 and so on. All processes arrive at time 0, so turnaround time = completion time and waiting time = turnaround − burst.
